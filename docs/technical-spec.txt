1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In heavy rigging and industrial installation engineering, hydraulic gantry systems
(such as Lift Systems, Greiner, and Enerpac modular units) frequently operate near
both their mechanical hoist limits and the structural capacity of the host facility
floor slab. A routine point-load lifting operation can result in severe slab
cracking, punching failure, or tower buckling if eccentricities, dynamic hoisting
spikes, and deadweight distributions are neglected.

The Hydraulic Gantry & Track Bearing Load Analyzer is a single-screen static
analysis and verification tool developed to eliminate manual calculation errors
during preliminary lift planning. It computes:

  1. Split vertical reactions across dual gantry towers under variable hook
     configurations (Config 1: Single Hook Point Load; Config 2: Dual Hook 50/50 Split).
  2. Progressive deadweight accumulation across header lifting beams, crown headers,
     side-shift/swivel assemblies, hydraulic telescopic legs, and gantry tracks.
  3. Internal beam shear force V(x) and bending moment M(x) diagrams evaluated via
     Heaviside step functions across 60 equidistant intervals.
  4. Actual ground contact bearing pressure ($q_{\text{actual}}$ in $\text{kN/m}^2$)
     based on a directional $45^\circ$ longitudinal dispersion model and zero
     transverse dispersion.
  5. Three-tier utilization gating:
     - Tier 1: Lifting Beam Safe Capacity Ceiling
     - Tier 2: Gantry Cylinder Extension Stage Capacity (Option A vs. Option B)
     - Tier 3: Allowable Floor & Slab Bearing Stress Limits


2. STRUCTURAL MECHANICS & MATHEMATICAL FORMULATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 Applied Suspended Load & Dynamic Amplification
The total applied hook force incorporates the dynamic hoisting factor ($\phi$) to
account for dynamic inertia, sling elongation, and hydraulic control pulsing:

$$P_{\text{dyn}} = P \cdot \phi \cdot \frac{g}{1000} \quad [\text{kN}]$$

Where:
  - $P$    = Gross suspended payload ($\text{kg}$)
  - $\phi$ = Hoisting / dynamic factor (dimensionless, typically $1.00\text{ to }1.25$
             pursuant to EN 13001-2 / DIN 15018)
  - $g$    = $9.80665\ \text{m/s}^2$ (standard gravity)


2.2 Beam-Level Statics (Reactions at Crown Supports)

Configuration 1: Single Hook Point Load
For an eccentric single hook located at distance $x$ from the center of Support A
along clear span $L$:

$$R_{\text{hook}, A} = P_{\text{dyn}} \cdot \left(\frac{L - x}{L}\right)$$
$$R_{\text{hook}, B} = P_{\text{dyn}} \cdot \left(\frac{x}{L}\right)$$

Configuration 2: Dual Hook (Equal 50/50 Split)
When rigging with a spreader frame or dual hoist blocks separated by distance
$L_{\text{spread}}$, centered at $x_{\text{center}}$ (constrained such that
$0 \le x_1 \le x_2 \le L$):

$$x_1 = x_{\text{center}} - \frac{L_{\text{spread}}}{2}, \quad x_2 = x_{\text{center}} + \frac{L_{\text{spread}}}{2}$$
$$P_1 = P_2 = \frac{P_{\text{dyn}}}{2}$$
$$R_{\text{hook}, A} = \frac{P_{\text{dyn}}}{2} \cdot \left[\frac{(L - x_1) + (L - x_2)}{L}\right]$$
$$R_{\text{hook}, B} = \frac{P_{\text{dyn}}}{2} \cdot \left[\frac{x_1 + x_2}{L}\right]$$

Header Lifting Beam Deadweight Distribution
The self-weight of the lifting beam ($W_{\text{beam}}$ in $\text{kN}$) is treated
as an ideal uniformly distributed load ($w_{\text{beam}} = \frac{W_{\text{beam}}}{L}$),
distributing equally to each crown header:

$$R_{\text{beam}, A} = R_{\text{beam}, B} = \frac{W_{\text{beam}}}{2} = \frac{m_{\text{beam}} \cdot g}{2 \cdot 1000}$$

Total Crown Reaction Force ($F_{\text{crown}}$)
The total mechanical reaction applied directly to the top of each hydraulic
gantry tower is:

$$F_{\text{crown}, A} = R_{\text{hook}, A} + R_{\text{beam}, A} \quad [\text{kN}]$$
$$F_{\text{crown}, B} = R_{\text{hook}, B} + R_{\text{beam}, B} \quad [\text{kN}]$$


2.3 Internal Forces: Shear Force & Bending Moment Distributions
The lifting beam internal shear force $V(x)$ and bending moment $M(x)$ along the
longitudinal axis ($0 \le x \le L$) are evaluated using piecewise equilibrium
equations:

$$V(x) = R_{\text{crown}, A} - (w_{\text{beam}} \cdot x) - \sum_{i} P_i \cdot \mathcal{H}(x - x_i)$$
$$M(x) = R_{\text{crown}, A} \cdot x - \frac{1}{2} w_{\text{beam}} \cdot x^2 - \sum_{i} P_i (x - x_i) \cdot \mathcal{H}(x - x_i)$$

Where $\mathcal{H}(x - x_i)$ is the Heaviside step function:

$$\mathcal{H}(x - x_i) = \begin{cases} 0 & x < x_i \\ 1 & x \ge x_i \end{cases}$$

The analyzer samples these equations at 60 equidistant discrete intervals across
the span (plus exact point-load ordinates) to render the interactive Shear Force
Diagram (SFD) and Bending Moment Diagram (BMD).


3. FLOOR LOADING & LONGITUDINAL 45° DISPERSION PHYSICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Cumulative Vertical Base Force
The total vertical force exerted by each gantry assembly onto its supporting track
and foundation accumulates deadweights that do not stress the header beam:

$$F_{\text{base}, i} = F_{\text{crown}, i} + W_{\text{crown\_dead}} + W_{\text{tracks\_tower}} + W_{\text{track\_unit}} \quad [\text{kN}]$$

Where:
  - $W_{\text{crown\_dead}}$ = Combined weight of hydraulic crown header, side-shift
                               cylinders, and top swivel bearings per tower ($\text{kN}$).
  - $W_{\text{tracks\_tower}}$ = Weight of telescopic stages, main cylinder barrels,
                                 propulsion drives, and chassis shoe per tower ($\text{kN}$).
  - $W_{\text{track\_unit}}$ = Track self-weight plus user-entered hardwood
                               support mass, when selected, per tower ($\text{kN}$).


3.2 Ground Contact Area & Dispersion Geometry
The floor-bearing model starts from the loaded contact footprint at the top face of
the concrete slab. The gantry track either bears directly on the slab or transfers
load through an editable array of hardwood blocks. Longitudinal dispersion is
modelled at $45^\circ$ through the concrete slab thickness; rail depth is not added
to the slab spread depth.

Direct track-to-slab mode:

    Gantry shoe (L_base) -> track contact footprint at slab top
    ------------------------------  <- concrete slab top
      \                            /
       \  45° spread in slab      /
        \                        /
    ==============================  <- concrete slab bottom
    [<--------------- L_eff ------>]

$$L_{\text{eff}} = L_{\text{base}} + 2 \cdot h_{\text{slab}} \quad [\text{mm}]$$

The editable slab-thickness input defaults to $200\text{ mm}$. The 45-degree
boundaries begin at the loaded footprint on the slab top and end at the slab
bottom.

Hardwood array mode:

$1000$ to $1600\text{ mm}$ hardwood block lengths are available in
$100\text{ mm}$ increments. This is the transverse dimension shown in the frontal
view, and the selected length must exceed the track width. The app selects the
smallest available preset greater than the selected track width when the current
selection is too short. If no preset exceeds the track width, hardwood bearing
results are withheld.

The timber profile is shown in longitudinal side view: the profile width runs
along the rail and defaults to $200\text{ mm}$; profile height defaults to
$100\text{ mm}$. Profile width is editable in $100\text{ mm}$ increments. The
block count is limited so multiple profiles fit within the shoe length, and the
clear gap is reduced if required. Block count, clear gap, and timber mass per
support are also editable. Timber mass is included in the base force when
supplied. These settings are configuration controls, not a specified company
hardwood standard.

Each block footprint along the rail is its profile width, $w_{\text{profile}}$.
It is expanded by $h_{\text{slab}}$ on both longitudinal sides. Overlapping
expanded intervals are merged, and their total union length defines $L_{\text{eff}}$:

$$I_i = [x_i, x_i + w_{\text{profile}}], \qquad
I_i' = [x_i - h_{\text{slab}}, x_i + w_{\text{profile}} + h_{\text{slab}}]$$

$$L_{\text{eff}} = \operatorname{length}\left(\bigcup_i I_i'\right) \quad [\text{mm}]$$

Transverse Dimension ($W_{\text{eff}}$) — Zero Transverse Spread

No lateral spread is assumed. In direct mode, $W_{\text{eff}} = W_{\text{track}}$;
in hardwood mode, $W_{\text{eff}}$ is the transverse hardwood block length, which
must be greater than $W_{\text{track}}$:

$$W_{\text{eff}} = \begin{cases}
W_{\text{track}} & \text{direct track-to-slab} \\
l_{\text{block}} & \text{hardwood block array}
\end{cases} \quad [\text{mm}]$$

Contact Bearing Area ($A_{\text{eff}}$) and Pressure ($q$)

$$A_{\text{eff}} = \frac{L_{\text{eff}} \cdot W_{\text{eff}}}{1\,000\,000} \quad [\text{m}^2]$$
$$q_A = \frac{F_{\text{base}, A}}{A_{\text{eff}}} \quad \left[\frac{\text{kN}}{\text{m}^2}\right], \qquad q_B = \frac{F_{\text{base}, B}}{A_{\text{eff}}} \quad \left[\frac{\text{kN}}{\text{m}^2}\right]$$


4. MULTI-TIER STRUCTURAL VERIFICATION & GATING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The software performs three concurrent structural checks (plus an eccentric load
asymmetry check), flagging any violation with real-time UI banners and color-coded
status warnings (`PASS <= 85%`, `CAUTION 85–100%`, `OVERLOAD > 100%`):

Tier 1: Lifting Beam Safe Capacity Ceiling
  - Primary Check (Default): $\max(F_{\text{crown}, A}, F_{\text{crown}, B}) \le \text{Capacity}_{\text{beam\_rated}}$
  - Secondary Basis Toggle: Total Factored Payload $P_{\text{dyn}} \le \text{Capacity}_{\text{beam\_rated}}$ (Gross SWL)
  - Criteria: Reaction forces at beam ends (or gross factored hook load) must not
    exceed the pre-approved manufacturer Safe Working Load (SWL) for the selected
    profile. Reserve margin is displayed in both $\text{kN}$ and $\text{kg}$.
  - Note: This check is treated as a hard ceiling. Rigging engineers must verify
    unbraced compression flange lengths against lateral torsional buckling (LTB)
    when using long spans.

Tier 2: Gantry Cylinder Extension Check (Option A vs. Option B)
  - Option A (Active by Default): Evaluates $F_{\text{crown}, A}$ and $F_{\text{crown}, B}$
    against the manufacturer's 2-point lift rating for the selected cylinder stage
    (`Retracted`, `Stage 1`, `Stage 2`, or `Stage 3`).
    If a unit has no published 2-point rating (e.g., Greiner 620XL T or Lift Systems
    620T II in certain multi-stage configurations), the software marks it as
    `NO PUBLISHED 2-PT RATING` (`KEINE 2-PKT.-FREIGABE`) and does not assume an
    arbitrary 50% split.
  - Option B (Bypass Mode): Bypasses cylinder stage verification for preliminary
    equilibrium framing or when 4-point coupled synchronized lifts are verified via
    independent manufacturer chart software.

Tier 3: Floor & Slab Bearing Stress Check
  - Check: $\max(q_A, q_B) \le q_{\text{allowable}}$
  - Criteria: Contact pressure must remain below the engineer-verified slab capacity
    (typically $30\text{ to }250\ \text{kN/m}^2$ for industrial slabs). If
    $q > q_{\text{allowable}}$, a violation alert triggers and calculates the minimum
    required load-spreading mat area per tower:
    $$A_{\text{mat,min}} = \frac{\max(F_{\text{base}, A}, F_{\text{base}, B})}{q_{\text{allowable}}} \quad [\text{m}^2]$$

Eccentric Load Distribution Warning
  - Check: Triggers when either support carries $> 70\%$ of the total crown reaction
    ($F_{\text{crown}, i} / (F_{\text{crown}, A} + F_{\text{crown}, B}) > 0.70$),
    reminding operators to verify synchronous cylinder extension control and
    differential slab settlement.


5. SOFTWARE ARCHITECTURE, LOCALIZATION & IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Runtime Environment:
  - Client-side HTML5, vanilla JavaScript (ES6+), and CSS3 (Tailwind CSS via CDN).
  - Zero backend or server dependencies; runs directly in modern browsers.
  - Diagrams powered by dynamic inline SVG (Free-Body & 45° Dispersion Schematic)
    and Chart.js (60-interval SFD & BMD).

Project File Structure:
  - `index.html`            : Application shell, sidebar inputs, 3-tier verification
                              cards, interactive SVG canvas, Chart.js view, and
                              progressive equilibrium breakdown table.
  - `data/gantry-units.js`  : `INVENTORY_GANTRIES` catalog (unit weights, base shoe
                              dimensions $L_{\text{base}} \times W_{\text{base}}$, and
                              4-stage pin heights / 2-point SWL ratings).
  - `data/gantry-tracks.js` : `INVENTORY_TRACKS` catalog (track profile depth
                              $h_{\text{track}}$, flange width $W_{\text{track}}$, and
                              rail section mass).
  - `data/beams.js`         : `INVENTORY_BEAMS` catalog (25 header lifting beams with
                              clear span $L$, cross-section $W \times H$, self-weight,
                              and rated SWL).
  - `css/styles.css`        : Custom dark-slate UI rules, custom number input styling,
                              CAD schematic sheet styles, and `@media print` rules.
  - `js/tailwind-config.js` : Tailwind CSS theme and brand color configuration.
  - `js/app.js`             : Core statics engine, UI synchronization, SVG schematic
                              renderer, and Chart.js internal force plotter.
  - `docs/technical-spec.txt`: Technical White Paper & Design Methodology (this document).

Bilingual Interface (EN / DE):
  - Built-in runtime language switcher supporting English (`EN`) and German (`DE`).
  - Translates all 120+ static labels, dynamic verification badges, Chart.js axes
    (`Biegemoment M(x)` / `Querkraft V(x)`), SVG schematic annotations, equilibrium
    breakdown rows, and Section 6 boundary conditions using standard German structural
    engineering terminology (DIN 15018 / EN 13001-2).

Interactive Input & Schematic UX:
  - Non-Intrusive Numeric Input Handling: Center-aligned numeric fields with custom
    dark-theme SVG chevron steppers. Differentiates active typing (`oninput`) from
    committed values (`onchange`/`onblur`) so multi-digit numbers can be typed
    freely without premature minimum-value clamping.
  - Interactive CAD Schematics (Right-Side Portal FBD + Section-Specific Input Schematics):
    1. Right Column — `GA-01` Free-Body Structural Schematic (Frontal Portal View):
       Reserved alongside the report calculation cards/tables. Renders Tower A, Tower B,
       and the header lifting beam in Frontal Elevation. Because the tracks run
       perpendicular to the portal plane (into the page), the contact patch width seen in
       the frontal view equals the track flange width ($W_{\text{eff}} = W_{\text{track}} \approx W_{\text{base}}$)
       with $0^\circ$ lateral dispersion (vertical $90^\circ$ projection edges, no $45^\circ$
       transverse flare). Supports direct pointer dragging of the hook/spreader ($x$),
       clear span ($L$), frontal shoe width ($W_{\text{base}}$), and track depth ($h_{\text{track}}$).
    2. Left Column Section 2 — Simplified Hydraulic Gantry Tower Schematic (Frontal View):
       Embedded directly in Section 2 (`2. Hydraulic Gantry Towers & Deadweights`),
       depicting only the tower unit (without tracks) following the manufacturer frontal
       elevation CAD drawing: dashed header beam at top, side-shift/swivel pad ($W_{\text{shift}}$),
       twin-pin crown header ($W_{\text{crown}}$), twin stepped telescopic cylinders, left
       vertical pin-height dimension ($H_{\text{stage}}$), lower housing with control panel
       and indicator slots ($W_{\text{tower}}$), and 4-wheel bogie shoe ($W_{\text{base}}$
       frontal primary, with $L_{\text{base}}$ noted as secondary into the page).
    3. Left Column Section 3 — Gantry Track & $45^\circ$ Dispersion Schematic:
       Embedded directly in Section 3 (`3. Gantry Tracks & 45° Dispersion Physics`),
       separating the track cross-section ($W_{\text{track}} \times h_{\text{track}}$ with
       $0^\circ$ lateral spread and rail mass $W_{\text{rail}}$) on the left from the
       longitudinal side view ($L_{\text{base}}$ sitting on $h_{\text{track}}$ with $45^\circ$
       longitudinal dispersion $L_{\text{eff}} = L_{\text{base}} + 2 \cdot h_{\text{track}}$,
       $A_{\text{eff}}$, and $q_{\text{allowable}}$) on the right.
  - Scenario Presets & PDF Export: Includes quick presets (`180t Rotor (Dual)`,
    `85t Trafo (Single)`, `50t Press`) and print-optimized stylesheet formatting for
    generating PDF engineering reports.

Hosting Interoperability:
  - Compatible with GitHub Pages, Azure Static Web Apps, local file execution, and

6. ENGINEERING LIMITATIONS & BOUNDARY CONDITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Users must recognize the following boundary constraints when utilizing this analyzer:

  1. Quasi-Static 2D Model:
     The analyzer models 2D planar equilibrium ($\Sigma F_z = 0, \Sigma M = 0$). It
     does not compute 3D lateral crabbing, track misalignments, side-pull, or wind
     forces.
  2. Track Span Continuity:
     Calculations assume track sections are continuously supported by a uniform, flat
     slab. If tracks span across floor pits, drainage trenches, or expansion joints,
     rails must be engineered as unsupported bridge spans.
  3. Rigid Base & Zero Lateral Spread Assumption:
     Ground pressure is calculated as an average equivalent contact stress
     ($q = F_{\text{base}} / A_{\text{eff}}$) with $45^\circ$ longitudinal and $0^\circ$
     transverse dispersion. Edge stress concentrations due to base tilting or elastic
     soil-structure interaction are not resolved.
  4. Sign-Off Requirement:
     This tool serves as a preliminary lift planning aid and does not supersede
     signed and stamped lift studies executed by a licensed Professional Engineer
     (PE / Prüfingenieur).
